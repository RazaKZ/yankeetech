Planning Your SaaS MVP Budget

"Define your first release, understand the starting budget and plan the handover."

Start with one useful workflow

A useful first release gives a specific customer a way to complete a specific task. Before choosing features, describe that task, the people involved and what a successful result looks like.

What goes into the budget?

Scope includes the interface, user accounts, business rules, integrations, testing and deployment. A payment flow or a complex permission model can add work even when the screen looks simple.

Our project estimator starts a Starter SaaS MVP at $2,200 before optional features. Treat the result as an initial planning range. We confirm the price after reviewing your workflows, data and integration requirements.

Separate launch essentials from later additions

Write down what a customer must be able to do on day one. Keep reporting enhancements, additional integrations and secondary workflows in a follow-up list. This gives your team a clear acceptance checklist for the first release.

Plan for the handover

Include hosting, third-party subscriptions, documentation and maintenance in your planning. Agree who owns the accounts and source code, who handles support and how future changes will be scoped.

Prepare a useful project brief

Share your customer journey, example screens, existing tools and preferred launch date. If you already have a prototype or codebase, include it in the review so the estimate reflects the work that remains.
